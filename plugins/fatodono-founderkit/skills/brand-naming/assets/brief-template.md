# Naming Brief

Fill this before generating a single candidate. It is short on purpose — every line is a constraint
that will do work later. If you can't answer a line, write your best guess and mark it `[assumed]`
so the user can correct it.

---

## What we're naming

- **The thing:** <product / company / feature / library>
- **Role in the architecture:** <master brand | product under parent | feature | codename>
  <!-- A feature under a strong parent can afford to be descriptive. A master brand almost never can. -->
- **Category as it exists today:** <what a customer would say this is>
- **Markets / languages that matter:** <list — this drives Gate 3 screening>

## The Diamond

1. **What does winning look like?**
   <Five years out, if this works, what is true? Not "a good name" — a picture.>

2. **What do we have to win with?**
   <Real assets: technology, founder story, an invented category, an unfair advantage.>

3. **What do we need to win?**
   <The gap. Credibility? Approachability? Permission to charge more? Bravery?>

4. **What do we need to say?**
   <The single idea a person should walk away with. Usually not a feature.>

## Sound strategy

Decide how the name should *sound* before deciding what it should mean.

- **Fast or full?**
- **Sharp or soft?**
- **Light or heavy?**
- **Reliable or daring?**
- **Warm or precise?**

**Target phonetics:** <e.g. "initial stop for recall, front vowels for speed, avoid sibilant
clusters" — see references/sound-symbolism.md>

**Reference brands whose *sound* is right** (even from unrelated categories):
<3-5 names, and what specifically about their sound you're after>

## Constraints that are real

- **Hard constraints:** <legal, existing brand architecture, regulatory>
- **Soft preferences the user stated:** <length, style, "no made-up words">
  <!-- Record these, then challenge the ones that will hurt: length caps, .com-must-be-free,
       "everyone on the team has to like it." -->
- **Explicitly out of bounds:** <competitor territory, prior failed candidates and why>

## What this name must not forbid

<What business lines, geographies, or product categories does the company want available in five
years? This is the question that kills descriptive names — TripActions couldn't sell a corporate
card.>

## Displaced brief (for the blind pass)

**We are naming:** <an unrelated thing whose *feeling* matches the sound strategy — a sailboat, a
Japanese knife brand, a jazz label, an alpine weather station, a cartography studio>

## Rival brief (for the competitor pass)

**A competitor is launching next month and will take this market. Name them.**

## Success criteria for the final name

Restate as a checklist you'll score finalists against, drawn from the Diamond above:

- [ ] Delivers <the one idea>
- [ ] Sounds <the sound strategy>
- [ ] Survives <the five-year expansion>
- [ ] Distinct from <named competitors>
- [ ] Spellable on first hearing
- [ ] Pre-screens clean in <markets>
