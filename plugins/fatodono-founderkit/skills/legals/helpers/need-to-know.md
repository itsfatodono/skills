# Need-to-know

How much a legal document says. `doc-writer` writes to it; `overshare-checker` checks against it.

A document says what its skeleton's patterns call for, at the level each section's *Detail* sets
(or, without one, its majority pattern). A fact in `legal/facts.md` is evidence that a sentence
is true, never a reason to write one: a detail past the level is a promise the code breaks on its
next change, and some show an attacker the way in.

## The level

- **Data:** categories, with examples.
- **Safeguards:** general terms.
- **Processing:** what happens to the data and where: on the device, on a server, in which
  country.
- **Services:** each service in `facts.product.services_in_scope`, named by what it does ("the
  organiser panel").
- **Short-lived technical records:** one retention category, with their longest period.
- **Facts:** a section draws on the keys its *Facts* names, in its own file or a region file.

## What the law asks

Each data category, purpose, recipient and period, how to use each right, and who the
controller or the parties are: need-to-know however they are worded, and each keeps its section.
When one reads too detailed, it is rewritten at the level.

## The company's identity

The postal address, registry numbers and KEP are written out once: in the skeleton's `contact`
section, or, when it has none, its `controller` or `parties` section. Every other section, the
regional part included, points to that section by its heading, also where the reader needs the
address for a step ("write to us at the address under *Contact us*"). The opening names the
legal entity as controller or party.

Four things repeat by design: the legal name; the contact email, as the channel of the step a
section describes; a form the reader fills in and sends (a model withdrawal form), with the name
and address it is sent to; and a signature block.

## Copied text

Text a document copies (an official template's fixed text, a statute's wording, a licence) is
the issuer's, and is copied as it stands.
