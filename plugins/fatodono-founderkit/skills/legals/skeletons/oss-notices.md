## Document

- **Purpose:** Lists the open-source and other third-party components shipped in the product, with
  their licences and the notices those licences ask the distributor to pass on, and says how to get
  source code where a licence requires it.
- **Shape:**
  - 9/10 one web page on the company's site; 1/10 a help-center article. None is split across
    pages. Median about 17,800 words (range 105–462,931): the length comes from licence texts
    repeated per component, not from the company's own words.
  - 6/10 a block per component (name, then notice or licence text); 3/10 a table, one row per
    component, with the licence texts in an appendix or per row; 1/10 a short web summary that
    sends the reader into the app for the full list.
  - 5/10 group the components by platform, build target or product surface (iOS, Android, web,
    desktop module); 5/10 give one flat list, 3 of them alphabetical.
  - 5/10 are generated by a dependency tool (2 name it on the page); 5/10 are kept by hand and
    show it (inconsistent names, stale entries, missing separators).
- **Register:** almost no company voice. 4/10 have no text of their own below the title, 3/10 one
  or two sentences, 1/10 a short header and a source-code offer, 1/10 a formal legal preamble with
  defined terms in capitals, 1/10 a technical note addressed to maintainers. Everything else is
  component data and licence text copied as shipped.
- **Recurring elements:**
  - A date: 3/10 (a generation timestamp, "Last reviewed", "Last updated").
  - Counts of components or licences, or a contents list: 3/10.
  - A credit line for the tool that generated the list: 1/10.
- **Commonly omitted:** a date (7/10); an opening that says what the list is (4/10); a source-code
  offer (9/10, including 2 lists that contain copyleft components); a contact for licence
  requests (8/10); component versions (6/10).

## Sections

Region-neutral. No sample gives content that one law requires; the source-code offer and NOTICE
files come from the components' licences and stay here. One sample adds a French copy of its
preamble (`research/oss-notices/merge.md` *Regional content*).

### scope: About these notices

- **Covers:** that the product contains third-party open-source software, which products,
  platforms or builds the notices cover, what each entry holds, and that each component is used
  under its own licence.
- **Prevalence:** 6/10 (60%), common
- **Applies when:** always
- **Facts:** `facts.product.name`, `facts.product.services_in_scope`, `facts.company.legal_name`, `facts.product.third_party_components`
- **Patterns:**
  - 5/6 one sentence under the title; 1/6 a dense legal paragraph with defined terms in capitals.
  - 2/6 use one stock sentence ("the following sets forth attribution notices for third party
    software that may be contained in portions of…"), hedged with "may".
  - 1/6 gives the total number of components; 1/6 says which dependencies are included
    (transitive ones in, development-only ones out).
  - 2/10 say the components' own licences govern them, not the company's terms; 1/10 adds that
    any terms the company offers on top come from the company alone.
  - 2/10 state the limits of the list: it may be incomplete or wrong, it is not legal advice, and
    readers may report errors.
  - 4/10 have no opening at all: the title is the only framing.
- **Quote:** "Each component is used under the license shown; the full text of the common licenses is reproduced at the end of this page." (s05)
- **Commonly omitted:** which platforms or builds are covered (named by 3/6); a date.

### source-offer: Source code for copyleft components

- **Covers:** how to get the source code of copyleft components the product ships: a written offer
  or a download, where requests go and what they must contain, for how long and at what cost.
- **Prevalence:** 1/10 (10%), rare
- **Applies when:** `facts.product.third_party_components.copyleft == true`
- **Facts:** `facts.product.source_code_offer`, `facts.company.contact_email`, `facts.company.address`
- **Patterns:**
  - 1/1 uses the written-offer form: complete machine-readable source, valid three years from the
    last shipment, to any recipient, free except the cost of media and shipping. It opens its
    platform's part, before the list, and points to the copyleft licence text.
  - 1/1 gives an email and a postal address, and a short list of what a request must include
    (product and version, the component, a return address).
  - 1/10 gives a request channel with no offer (a postal address marked for open-source requests,
    the same request list) in its opening.
  - 2/10 list copyleft components with no offer for them (one platform part of the sample above,
    and a list with LGPL-3.0 and MPL-2.0 entries).
- **Avoid:** listing copyleft components shipped in binary form with neither a source download nor
  a written offer (2/10); GPL-2.0 §3, LGPL-2.1 §6 and LGPL-3.0 §4 make one of them a condition of
  distribution.
- **Quote:** "This offer is valid for a period of three (3) years from the date of our last shipment of this product, and is valid to any recipient of this information." (s01)
- **Commonly omitted:** a direct link to the source (0/1 gives one); a web form.

### warranty-disclaimer: No warranty for the components

- **Covers:** that the open-source components come without warranty or liability from the company
  or their authors.
- **Prevalence:** 2/10 (20%), rare
- **Applies when:** `facts.product.third_party_components.copyleft == true`
- **Facts:** `facts.product.third_party_components`
- **Patterns:**
  - 1/2 uses the GNU wording ("distributed in the hope that it will be useful, but WITHOUT ANY
    WARRANTY") inside the source-code offer, for the LGPL parts.
  - 1/2 gives one sentence in the opening that disclaims on behalf of the company and the
    components' authors together.
  - Every licence text in `licence-texts` carries its own disclaimer; this section states it once
    for the reader.
- **Quote:** "Lyft and the third-party authors, licensors, and distributors of the MATERIALS disclaim all warranties and liability arising from all use and distribution of the MATERIALS." (s04)
- **Commonly omitted:** any limit of liability beyond the disclaimer.

### component-list: Components and their licences

- **Covers:** each third-party component shipped to the user (libraries, fonts, models, design
  assets, vendor SDKs) with the licence it is used under.
- **Prevalence:** 10/10 (100%), core
- **Applies when:** always
- **Facts:** `facts.product.third_party_components`
- **Patterns:**
  - Unit: 7/10 one entry per component; 1/10 one entry per licence, packages named in brackets;
    1/10 one block per identical licence text; 1/10 one line per licence type with a count and no
    component named, the full list being in the app.
  - Fields: name 9/10; licence named as a label 8/10 (an SPDX id in 4/10); copyright line 7/10;
    project or repository link 5/10; version 4/10. 2/10 name no licence, so the reader finds it
    only in the text.
  - Grouping: 5/10 by platform, build target or product surface, 1/10 also by licence inside each
    part; 5/10 one flat list. 3/10 put a compact index (name and licence) before the texts.
  - Beyond code libraries: fonts or design assets 4/10; AI models 2/10 (base model, source
    revision, training-data licence, changes made, use restrictions); proprietary or
    source-available components in the same list 4/10, only 1 of them marked as not open source.
  - 4/10 include transitive dependencies; 4/10 keep a component's own upstream notice whole inside
    its entry (a bundled library list, subcomponent notices).
  - 3/10 also list server-side components that never reach the user; leave them out: the list
    covers what is shipped.
  - 1/10 says how the list is kept current; 1/10 is visibly stale (copyright years end a decade
    back, names differ between index and entries).
  - Size runs from 13 to about 3,000 components.
- **Quote:** "Licensed under the BSD 3-Clause License." (s09)
- **Commonly omitted:** versions (6/10); which platform uses each component (5/10); copyright lines
  (3/10).

### other-locations: Other copies of these notices

- **Covers:** where else the notices are kept: an in-app screen, plain-text files, copies inside
  the download or container.
- **Prevalence:** 2/10 (20%), rare
- **Applies when:** `facts.product.third_party_components.other_copies` is not empty
- **Facts:** `facts.product.third_party_components`
- **Patterns:**
  - 1/2 links plain-text copies (the full notice and an aggregate of NOTICE files) and names the
    copies inside the shipped container, near the top and again in the footer.
  - 1/2 is a web summary that sends the reader to a menu path in the app for the full list and
    licence texts.
- **Quote:** "See Help → View Dependency Licenses in Zed for full license text." (s06)
- **Commonly omitted:** which copy is the current one.

### licence-texts: Licence texts

- **Covers:** the full text of each licence the components are under, with its exceptions, and the
  copyright lines the licence asks to keep.
- **Prevalence:** 9/10 (90%), core
- **Applies when:** always
- **Facts:** `facts.product.third_party_components`
- **Patterns:**
  - 6/10 put the text inside each component's entry, so a common licence repeats many times;
    3/10 give each licence once in an appendix linked from the list; 1/10 a collapsible excerpt per
    row plus a downloadable text file.
  - 4/10 give some entries by reference only (an SPDX id, a link to the LICENSE file, the short
    Apache header with the licence URL).
  - 1/10 adds plain-language summaries of what each licence family allows or requires, with a note
    that the full text governs.
  - 1/10 shows only one option's text for a dual-licensed component.
- **Avoid:** generic licence texts with the copyright line dropped or left as a placeholder (3/10);
  the MIT, BSD and ISC licences make keeping the copyright notice a condition.
- **Quote:** "This Font Software is licensed under the SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1." (s01)
- **Commonly omitted:** each component's own copyright line in appendix-style texts.

### notice-files: NOTICE files

- **Covers:** the contents of upstream NOTICE files, which the Apache License 2.0 asks
  redistributors to pass on, or which components carry one and where it is reproduced.
- **Prevalence:** 1/10 (10%), rare
- **Applies when:** a component in `facts.product.third_party_components` has `notice == true`
- **Facts:** `facts.product.third_party_components`
- **Patterns:**
  - 1/1 explains the duty in one sentence, links an aggregate NOTICE file and lists the packages
    whose NOTICE it holds, by build target.
  - 2/10 paste a component's NOTICE text inside its entry in `component-list` instead.
- **Quote:** "When redistributing Apache-licensed packages with a NOTICE file, you usually must retain it." (s03)
- **Commonly omitted:** the NOTICE texts on the page itself (1/1 links them).

### data-attributions: Data and content attributions

- **Covers:** attribution notices for third-party data or content that is not software (map data,
  datasets), including a supplier's required notice.
- **Prevalence:** 1/10 (10%), rare
- **Applies when:** `facts.product.data_attributions != none`
- **Facts:** `facts.product.data_attributions`
- **Patterns:**
  - 1/1 closes the page with one entry per source: the standard open map-data line (source,
    licence, licensor, links), and a proprietary supplier's notice (copyright years, rights
    reserved, a warning against copying, a link to the supplier's terms).
  - 1/10 gives a model's training-data licence inside the model's `component-list` entry instead.
- **Quote:** "Data by © OpenStreetMap contributors (openstreetmap.org/copyright), licensed under Open Data Commons Open Database License (opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/) by the OpenStreetMap Foundation (osmfoundation.org/)." (s04)
- **Commonly omitted:** where in the product the attribution is also shown.
