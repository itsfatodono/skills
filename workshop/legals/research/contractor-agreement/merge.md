# contractor-agreement: merged section list

Row-level mapping: `section-map.md`. Formats per `build.md` step 3: `prose`, `list`, `table`,
plus the layout marks `pointer` (the section points to an exhibit or another document) and
`embedded` (folded into another section, no heading of its own).

## First batch

14 valid tables: large s01 s04 s07 s10 (the large pool ran out: c13 c17 c21 c25 and the earlier
large texts carried departure terms); mid s08 s18 s22 s26 s31; small s03 s06 s09 s15 s19. Every
id below occurs in at least one of them.

## Section ids

Fixed after mapping the first batch and before judging any later sample. A contract's closing
clauses are one id (`general`), as in the terms of service. Changes after this point are listed
under *Changes*.

| Id | Meaning |
|---|---|
| parties | Opening: title, the parties (entity form, address), effective date, defined roles; recitals giving the company's business, the contractor's expertise and the purpose of the engagement; the consideration line. |
| services | What the contractor does: the engagement grant, scope of services and deliverables (in the body or an exhibit, statement of work or task order), how work is ordered under a master agreement, who personally performs, acceptance of deliverables. |
| work-arrangements | How, when and where the work is done: time commitment and availability, schedule, place and travel, reporting line, contact persons, approvals, the company's duty to cooperate and supply information, tools and equipment. |
| scope-changes | How the scope changes: change requests and change orders, estimates for additional work. |
| fees | What the contractor is paid and on what basis (hourly, daily, fixed, retainer, milestone, equity), increases, caps on billable time, "entire compensation" statements. |
| invoicing-payment | How and when money moves: invoices (cadence, content, time records), approval, payment days, method, late interest, suspension for non-payment, set-off, payment to date on termination when written in the payment clause. |
| expenses | Reimbursable expenses: which, prior approval and thresholds, receipts, travel rules, advances. |
| taxes | Who pays which taxes: the contractor's own income and social taxes, no withholding, tax forms, indirect taxes on fees. |
| independent-contractor | Status: not an employee, partner or agent; no employee benefits; control of method and hours; no authority to bind; the contractor's responsibility for its own staff; consequences of a status challenge. |
| term | When the agreement starts and ends: effective date, fixed term or until completion, renewal. |
| termination | Ending early: for convenience on notice, for cause with or without cure, on insolvency, death or disability; effect (pay to date, work in progress); survival. |
| return-of-materials | Return or destruction of the company's property and materials at the end or on request; certification. |
| confidentiality | Confidential information: definition, non-use and non-disclosure, standard of care, who may see it, exclusions, compelled disclosure, duration, third parties' information the company holds. |
| ip-ownership | Who owns the work product: work made for hire, assignment, invention disclosure and records, moral rights, further assurances and power of attorney, flow-down to the contractor's staff, waiver of claims against the company's use. |
| contractor-materials | The contractor's pre-existing or general materials, tools and know-how: what the contractor keeps, the licence the company gets when they are built into deliverables, third-party and open-source materials. |
| other-work | Exclusivity and the contractor's other work: freedom to work for others (and the company's to use others), limits on competing work during the term, conflicts with the contractor's existing duties (a university, a former employer), not bringing others' confidential information. |
| non-solicitation | Limits during or after the term on soliciting or hiring the company's staff, or soliciting its customers. |
| non-disparagement | Promises not to disparage the other party. |
| publicity | Use of the other party's name, logo or the engagement in publicity or a portfolio; approval of publications about the work. |
| data-protection | Personal data the contractor handles: processing on instructions, security, breach notice, a data processing or business associate agreement. |
| compliance | Laws, licences and policies the contractor must follow: compliance with law, permits and licences, work authorisation of its staff, company policies and site rules, anti-bribery. |
| warranties | Representations and warranties: authority, performance standard (professional, workmanlike), conformity of deliverables, non-infringement; warranty disclaimers; no guarantee of results. |
| indemnity | Indemnities and their procedure. |
| liability | Limitation of liability: excluded damages, caps, carve-outs. |
| insurance | Insurance either party must carry. |
| subcontracting | Who may do the work: subcontracting and delegation, consent, the contractor's personnel, flow-down of terms. |
| prior-work | Work done before the agreement: coverage of past activities, crediting fees already paid, settling earlier invoices, releases for past work. |
| disputes | How disputes are resolved: negotiation or mediation first, arbitration, jury waiver, equitable relief, attorneys' fees. |
| governing-law | Governing law and courts. |
| general | Closing clauses: entire agreement, amendment, waiver, severability, counterparts and e-signatures, headings and interpretation, assignment of the agreement, successors, notices, force majeure, currency, order of precedence, further assurances in general, acknowledgement of independent advice, no further commitment. |
| signatures | Execution blocks. |

Not section ids: `amend` marks an amendment's own preamble, recitals, ratification and signature
block when the exhibit files the agreement with later amendments (their substantive changes are
mapped to the ids they change); `doc` marks layout rows ("Intentionally deleted"). `reg-*` marks
content one law requires (*Regional content*).

**Order in the skeleton.** Median position, except where trailing exhibits pull an id's first
row late or a single clause pulls it early; then the pairwise order of the samples that have both
ids decides: `expenses` after `fees` (15/17) and `invoicing-payment` (9/12); `contractor-materials`
directly after `ip-ownership` (4/4); `taxes` after `independent-contractor` (10/13); `termination`
after `independent-contractor` (11/15); `non-solicitation` after `confidentiality` (5/6) and
`non-disparagement` beside it; `general` by the median of all its rows (0.74; its first row is
early at 0.56 because single clauses such as assignment, notices or definitions sit
mid-document), after `governing-law` (0.68).

**Tiers.** With 19 samples, core is 16/19 or more (84%); 15/19 is 79%, so `work-arrangements`,
`indemnity` and `disputes` are common.

## Folds (made at merge, after judging)

None changed a judgement or a count.

- Recitals, the engagement grant and "entire compensation" lines were never ids: recitals sit in
  `parties`, the grant in `services`, the compensation line in `fees`.
- Equitable relief and attorneys' fees inside confidentiality or covenant clauses are counted in
  `disputes` (one kind of fact: how a breach is enforced).
- Survival clauses are counted in `termination` wherever they sit.
- A caregiving excuse for missed deadlines (s24) and the definitions sections of the two
  cover-page forms are counted in `general`.

## IP: in the agreement, not a pointer (judgement call)

The sibling build `ip-assignment` covers a standalone invention and IP assignment. The contractor
skeleton keeps its own `ip-ownership` section: 11/19 samples carry the IP terms in the agreement
and 0/19 move the contractor's IP terms to a separate document (the only one that appends a
separate invention agreement, s12, was out of scope; s18 references a separate form only for the
contractor's subcontractors). The 8/19 without an IP clause are all advisory engagements with no
deliverable. The terms a contractor deal needs (licence or assignment per deliverable, the
contractor's own and open-source materials, `contractor-materials`) depend on facts of the
engagement that an employee-style assignment does not carry. `facts.contractor.ip_terms.placement`
lets a user who already uses a separate assignment drop the section.

## Document level

- Length: median about 3,000 words, range 1,208 (s24) to 10,386 (s01, with 15 amendments). By
  size: large median about 3,800 (4 samples), mid about 6,100, small about 2,100, standard forms
  1,208–6,973.
- Services in a separate part 12/19 (exhibit or schedule 9: s04 s06 s07 s08 s10 s18 s23 s27 s31;
  cover page 2: s16 s20; fill-in table 1: s24); body only 7/19 (s01 s03 s09 s15 s19 s22 s26).
- Master agreement with statements of work 3/19 (s07 s10 s20); cover page plus standard terms
  2/19 (s16 s20); letter agreement 1/19 (s10). Filed with later amendments 3/19 (s01 s18 s22);
  their own preambles and signature blocks are `amend`, not sections.
- Numbered sections 17/19 (s06 unnumbered; s26 partly). Recitals 11/19. Survival clause 11/19.
  Capitals for liability or jury clauses 4/19 (s03 s18 s23 s26). Definitions block 2/19 (s16
  s20). Plain short-sentence register 3/19 (s16 s20 s24).
- Whose paper: about 7/19 read as the consultant's paper (one-way company indemnity, liability
  limits that protect only the contractor: s03 s04 s06 s15 s19 s22 s26); the rest the company's.
  The skeleton's patterns keep both; `facts.contractor.liability` chooses.
- Equity as pay 9/19 (s01 s03 s06 s07 s19 s22 s23 s27 s31), a trait of small listed filers that
  pay advisers in stock.
