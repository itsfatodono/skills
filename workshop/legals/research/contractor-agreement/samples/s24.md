## Document
- Shape: a four-page fillable PDF (AcroForm, made in Word) titled "Freelance Work Agreement - SAMPLE", footer-dated 1/23/2025, linked as the "English" model contract from the issuer's live page `nyc.gov/site/dca/workers/workersrights/freelancer-workers.page` (a Spanish version sits beside it). The server answers a bare curl with 403; a browser User-Agent gets the PDF from the same URL. A one-sentence preamble, then two parts. Pages 1–2 hold the three numbered terms the law requires (1 Parties, 2 Scope of Work, 3 Payment) as labelled blanks, a services/rate table and check-boxes. Pages 3–4 hold nine headings marked "OPTIONAL:" (late payment, retainer, payment terms, duration/modification/termination, choice of law, limitations on liability, ownership, confidential information, general), short clauses with blanks and bracketed pick-one choices, then a two-party signature block. Margin and header notes in plain words tell the user what to put in each blank and when a term is worth including; they are part of the form.
- Register: plain-language guidance voice in the notes ("Be clear about the work to be done"); the clauses are short, third-person, with the capitalised roles "Freelance Worker" and "Hiring Party" and few defined terms ("Agreement", "Installments", "Confidential Information"). Written to protect the freelancer: the notes warn against arbitration, explain late fees and retainers, cap common-law liability at the contract value, excuse missed deadlines for illness or caregiving, and transfer IP only on final payment. Repeats that the parties should include only terms both understand. Silent on independent-contractor status, taxes, insurance, warranties, indemnity, non-solicit or non-compete, notices, assignment, severability and signature counterparts.
- Recurring elements: every page's footer carries "Page N of 4", a line "I have reviewed this page:" with initials blanks for both parties, and the form's date; optional headings in the form "OPTIONAL: <TOPIC>" in capitals; margin notes set beside the heading they explain.
- Parties: written for a hiring party (a person or business) that engages a freelance worker, an individual who may trade under a business name, for services of any kind (the notes' examples: writing an article, selling the right to a photo, developing a program, working set hours). Usable by either side; the notes speak to both. No other relationship between the parties is contemplated; a clean services engagement, so it passes the fit rule.
- Attachments: none attached. The General clause speaks of "any attachments" and the page-2 note says terms may also be agreed in emails or texts, but no exhibit, statement of work or schedule is included or named; the services table on page 2 serves as the scope.
