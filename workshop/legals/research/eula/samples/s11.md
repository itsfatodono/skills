## Document
- Shape: one long page under the site's Legal hub (left menu lists the other policies and terms). A friendly two-paragraph preface in the company's own voice, then the title "END USER LICENSE AGREEMENT" in caps, an untitled preamble, 14 numbered sections with bold titles ending in a full stop, most split into lettered sub-clauses (a), (b)…, then three unnumbered caps-labelled blocks (copyright, trademarks, consent). No table of contents.
- Register: two registers on one page. Preface is casual first person plural ("our lawyers have told us…", "We think you will find it quite reasonable"). The agreement itself is formal US contract style: defined terms in quotes and parentheses ("License Agreement", "Software", "Subscription", "Customer Data", "Software Services", "Service Suspensions", "Feedback"), company as "the Omni Group"/"we", user as "you"; warranty disclaimer and liability clauses in ALL CAPS.
- Recurring elements: click-wrap framing throughout ("Agree"/"Disagree" buttons named in preamble, fees clause and closing consent block); cross-references by section number (Section 4, Section 12); one product licence covers all the company's Mac apps, with both perpetual and subscription models handled by "If available and you licensed the Software on a Subscription basis" conditionals. No last-updated or effective date; only a copyright year range in the closing notice. Contact email appears only in the preface. Company postal address given twice (government-rights clause and copyright notice).
- Unusual: reverse engineering is expressly permitted except for two purposes; a 30-day money-back guarantee sits inside the warranty section; liability floor of a fixed small dollar amount.
