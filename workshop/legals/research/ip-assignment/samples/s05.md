## Document
- Shape: the exhibit is a signed offer letter (host) with three attachments: Exhibit A, a relocation assistance letter; Exhibit B, a "Confidentiality and Restrictive Covenant Agreement" (confidentiality with its own definition and exclusions, an 18-month non-solicit and non-disparagement, a 12-month non-compete, remedies, Illinois law and Chicago venue); Exhibit C, the "Proprietary Information and Invention Assignment Agreement". Only Exhibit C and the offer letter's pointer to it are extracted; Exhibits A and B carry no IP assignment. Exhibit C has 6 numbered sections, most split into lettered sub-clauses with run-in headings ("(b) Assignment of Inventions."), a two-column signature block, and its own Exhibit A (prior inventions).
- Register: first person from the employee ("I will", "I hereby assign"), the company in the third person; formal legal prose with long sentences and defined terms in quotes (Proprietary Information, Third Party Information, Inventions, Employee Inventions, Prior Inventions, Records, Instruments); the advice-of-counsel clause in capitals. The filed text has many scanning errors ("bad access", "now charts", "APls", "branch" for breach), so it was filed from a scanned signed copy.
- Recurring elements: cross-references by section number (2(b), 2(f), 1(b), 3); obligations stated as running "during the term of my employment ... and thereafter"; the company defined to include successors and assigns.
- Signer and context: an employee (a senior executive) signs; consideration listed in the preamble as employment, the equity opportunity and bonus opportunity, by reference to the offer letter. Host is an offer letter, which makes signing Exhibits B and C a condition of the offer. The letter's entire-agreement and acceptance sentences name only the offer letter and Exhibit B, not Exhibit C. Executed copy: names, dates and signatures filled in, both parties signed.
- Form origin: no "Form of" label, no law-firm footer or document number; the company's name appears in the text, not only in blanks. Title of a common US form type (PIIA); the offer letter calls it "Invention Assignment Agreement" and the exhibit label "Proprietary and Invention Assignment Agreement", so the three names differ. Section 4 uses a capitalised "Relationship" that the text never defines, a sign of a template adapted without its definitions. No Defend Trade Secrets Act notice (the agreement is dated before that Act), no moral-rights clause, no restrictive covenants (those sit in Exhibit B).
