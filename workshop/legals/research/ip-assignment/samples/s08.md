## Document
- Shape: the exhibit is a signed separation letter (host, 22 numbered sections) with two attachments: Exhibit A, a one-page "Renewal and Ratification of General Release and Waiver of Claims"; Exhibit B, the "Proprietary Information and Invention Assignment Agreement" the employee signed at hire in 2017. Extracted: Exhibit B whole, plus the host clauses that point to it or carve out of it (sections 1, 4, 10, 17 and item 1 of Exhibit A). Exhibit B has 7 numbered sections in decimal sub-clauses with run-in headings ("2.3 Assignment of Company Inventions."), a signature block with an advice-of-counsel line in capitals, and its own Exhibit A (prior inventions list plus the California § 2872 notice). The filing is page images with a hidden text layer; the text was read from that layer.
- Register: first person from the employee ("I hereby assign", "I will"), the company in the third person, often without the article ("Company"); formal prose with defined terms in quotes (Proprietary Information, Third Party Information, Invention, Intellectual Property Rights, Moral Rights, Prior Inventions, Company Inventions); cross-references by subsection title ("the subsection titled Government or Third Party") as well as by number. The host letter is second person ("you").
- Recurring elements: obligations stated as running "during and after" employment; the company defined to include subsidiaries, parent, affiliates, successors and assigns; California Labor Code § 2870 named in the assignment and the disclosure duty, § 2872 in the notice.
- Signer and context: an employee (a senior officer) signs; consideration in the preamble is employment or continued employment and the pay. Host is a separation agreement under which the employee acknowledges being bound by the attached agreement and its breach counts as "cause". Executed copy: names, 2017 signature dates and /s/ signatures filled in, both parties signed, company address given.
- Form origin: no "Form of" label; the assignment itself has no footer or document number, while every host page carries a law-firm-style document number ("US-DOCS\..."). The attachment's pages are numbered 4 to 8 and its heading is preceded by a stray "4.", so it was lifted from a longer hiring packet. Three names for it in one file: "Proprietary Information and Invention Assignment Agreement" (exhibit label), "Proprietary Information and Inventions Assignment Agreement" (heading), "Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Assignment Agreement" (its Exhibit A). The company name appears filled in, in capitals, in the preamble, under a former company name. The host mislabels the attachment (calls it Exhibit A in section 1, Exhibit B in section 4) and names a second agreement ("EIACA") that is not attached. No Defend Trade Secrets Act notice inside the assignment (the host's section 10 supplies one); no post-employment non-compete; no trailer clause presuming post-employment inventions to be the company's.
