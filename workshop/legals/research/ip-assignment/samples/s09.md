## Document
- Shape: short standalone deed: title, party lines, a two-item "Background" (lettered recitals), "It is agreed as follows", five numbered parts with headed sub-clauses (assignments, indemnities, notices, general, definitions and interpretation), then the execution block. Definitions come last, not first. No schedules, no confidentiality, non-compete or employment terms: it transfers one identified technology once, with a catch-all for anything not yet transferred.
- Register: formal Australian (New South Wales) commercial drafting: "deed", "novate", "trade marks", "in favour of", "full indemnity basis", "circuit layouts"; third person with capitalised defined terms ("Assignor", "Assignee", "Core Technology"). Careless in places: "The Assignors warrants", "for the benefit of the other parties" in a two-party deed, notices to addresses "set out below" that the deed never gives, "it's" for "its", a definition list of IP that runs to "all mental and human thoughts, ideas and intellect".
- Recurring elements: capitalised defined terms gathered in a closing definitions clause; numbered headed sub-clauses with lettered paragraphs; dated "Executed as a deed" line; signature blocks with name and date lines.
- Signer and context: the founder, as individual assignor, assigns his technology to his company; he also signs for the company as its president. Consideration as written is only "benefits granted to the Assignor", unspecified, and the assignor then confirms he is owed no compensation. The deed is confirmatory: it is dated two months after its effective and assignment date and refers to an earlier "Intellectual Property Assignment Agreement - FINAL" of that date. No host document: standalone exhibit.
- Form origin: an executed text, not a "Form of": real names, the product description and dates filled in, signatures as "/s/". No law-firm footer or document number. Boilerplate (notices by fax, deemed-receipt rules, novation, relationship of parties) reads as lifted from a generic Australian commercial-deed template, with parts that do not fit a two-party founder assignment left in.
