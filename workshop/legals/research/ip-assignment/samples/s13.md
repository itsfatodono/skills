## Document
- Shape: the exhibit is an executive offer letter (pages 1-5) with two attachments: Attachment 1, the Confidential Information and Invention Assignment Agreement (CIIAA), followed by Exhibit A (prior inventions list) and Exhibit B (California Labor Code § 2870); then Attachment 2, a Mutual Arbitration Agreement, which is not part of the sample. The CIIAA has no section numbers: bold run-in headings, grouped under three parent headings ("My Obligations to Pinterest", "Definitions", "General Provisions"), then a signature block and the two exhibits. The filing is page images with a hidden text layer; the text was read from that layer and spot-checked against the page images.
- Register: plain-English rewrite of a standard CIIAA: first person from the employee ("I'll", "I won't"), contractions, short sentences, Pinterest named throughout instead of "the Company"; legal terms kept where needed ("hereby assign", "works made for hire", "coupled with an interest") and one explicit gloss ("Legally speaking, I grant ..."). Leftovers of a numbered, formal source form: "section 3.4 below" and "Section 3(a)" point to sections that do not exist; one heading uses "Company" and "my employment by Company" while the rest says Pinterest; the agreement calls itself "this Confidential Information Agreement and Invention Assignment Agreement" once.
- Recurring elements: the defined "Relationship" (employment or consulting) as the period obligations run in, "during and after the Relationship"; capitalised defined terms (Confidential Information, Inventions, Company Inventions, Intellectual Property Rights, Copyright) defined at the end, not the start; "alone or with others" after every invention verb; headings phrased as imperatives ("Protect ...", "Inform ...", "Assist ...").
- Signer and context: an employee (here the incoming CFO) signs; the offer letter makes signing the CIIAA a condition to be met by the start date, and the CIIAA states the salary as compensation for works made for hire. Host document: the offer letter, which also holds a one-year post-employment staff non-solicit for employees outside California and a bullet barring use of others' trade secrets; neither assigns IP or points to the CIIAA beyond the rows below. Signed by the Chief People Officer for the company and by the employee, dated.
- Form origin: no "Form of" label, no law-firm footer or document number; the company's name is written into the text, not left as a blank. The dangling section references and the stray "Company" show it was rewritten from a numbered standard CIIAA. Exhibit B gives only California law, though the definition of Company Inventions speaks of "the applicable state law".
