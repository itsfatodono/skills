## Document
- Shape: two linked short agreements filed as two exhibits (EX1A-6, sequence 9 and 10). The first, "Intellectual Property Contribution and Assignment Agreement" (ipassignmentagreement.htm), is a title, a one-sentence party line, ten numbered clauses with run-in headings, a signature block and a closing "Exhibit A: Technology Assignment Agreement" heading with nothing under it. The exhibit is filed as its own file (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1742162/000174216221000016/ipassignmenttechn.htm): its own title, party line with a tax-treatment sentence, numbered clauses (assignment, consideration, further assurances, confidentiality, warranty, miscellaneous), signature block, and its own "Exhibit A Technology" describing what is assigned. No recitals, no definitions section beyond in-clause definitions, no state-law notice, no DTSA notice. An "Exhibit B" prior-inventions list is referenced in the first agreement but not filed.
- Register: formal US contract drafting, third person with capitalised defined terms ("Assignor", "Company", "Developer", "Intellectual Property"); long single-sentence clauses. Careless throughout: the first agreement switches to "the Purchaser" (undefined; carried over from the stock purchase agreement) in clauses 6 and 7, and mixes roman and letter enumeration ("(vi) ... and (c)"); the technology agreement's warranty runs "(i), (b), (c)". Word-to-EDGAR conversion leaves stray auto-numbers at page breaks ("3.", "8.", "2.", "6.", "9."), so the clause numbers as filed skip; the technology agreement's own cross-references ("this Section 3" in further assurances, "this Section 4" in confidentiality) show the original order 1 to 6.
- Recurring elements: effective date in each party line; defined terms in quotes and brackets; page numbers; "IN WITNESS WHEREOF" line then company and "Accepted and Agreed: ASSIGNOR" blocks; "/s/" signatures.
- Signer and context: the founder, as individual assignor ("Assignor" in the first agreement, "Developer" in the second), assigns to the company he heads; he also signs for the company as President/CEO. Given for shares: the first agreement names the consideration as the execution of a Founder Stock Purchase Agreement of the same date (filed as a separate exhibit, not part of this sample), the second as common stock issued under a stock purchase agreement, and states the assignment and share issue are meant to be tax-free. The technology agreement also points to a separate Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement of the same date (not filed in this exhibit). The first agreement's covenant speaks of "the Assignor's employment by the Company", though the signer is a founder.
- Form origin: an executed copy, not a "Form of": names, date and "/s/" signatures filled in. Signs of an unfinished template: a drafting note left in the consideration clause ("May use cash or other interest as consideration"), "including Description." as an unfilled placeholder and an empty documents list in the technology exhibit, "a Oregon cooperative corporation" with a Texas address, and two different governing laws (Oregon in the first agreement, New York in the second, with Texas courts). No law-firm footer or document number; the HTML titles are the Word file names ("IP Assignment Agreement.docx", "Exhibit A Technology Assignment Agreement.docx"). The technology agreement reads like a common US startup founder technology-assignment form.
