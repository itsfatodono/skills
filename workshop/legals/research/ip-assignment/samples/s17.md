## Document
- Shape: the exhibit is an executive offer letter (eleven numbered sections, signed) with three exhibits: Exhibit A, an Executive Severance and Change in Control Agreement (not part of the sample); Exhibit B, the Confidential Information and Invention Assignment Agreement (CIIAA); Exhibit C, a blank list of the officer's current outside business activities that belongs to the offer letter's conflicts-of-interest clause (not part of the sample). The CIIAA is an unheaded preamble, fifteen numbered paragraphs with bold run-in headings (paragraphs 2 and 4 have lettered sub-paragraphs), a capitalised acknowledgement over the signature block, then Appendix A (California Labor Code § 2870) and Appendix B (prior inventions list). The sample is the CIIAA with its appendices plus three offer-letter clauses that point to it.
- Register: formal first person from the employee ("I agree", "I hereby irrevocably assign"), long sentences, the company as "the Company" or "Company" (both, without the article, interchangeably). Drafting drift: cross-references switch between "paragraph" and "Section"; "without any further compensations"; a one-way form calls itself "this mutual agreement of the parties" in the confidentiality definition; Appendix A says the agreement does not apply to an "Assigned Invention" that qualifies under § 2870, although the definition already excludes those.
- Recurring elements: "without any further action required by either party" after each grant; defined terms set in place where first used (Assigned Inventions, Prior Inventions, Inventions, Confidential Information, Moral Rights), no definitions section; employment as the only relationship, with prior service deemed part of it; "forensically sound" retrieval and deletion repeated in two paragraphs.
- Signer and context: an employee signs (here the Chief Product Officer, on an offer of continued employment); the offer letter says all employees must sign the company's standard CIIAA, and the preamble calls the agreement a material part of the consideration for employment and says it records an agreement in force since employment began. Host document: the offer letter, whose clauses on the CIIAA are rows H1-H3; its at-will clause and arbitration clause cover the same ground as CIIAA paragraphs 15 and 11 without referring to them. The company signs as "Accepted and Agreed to" by its CEO.
- Form origin: no "Form of" label, no law-firm footer or document number. The offer letter calls it the company's "standard" CIIAA; the company's name and the employee's name are written in, not left blank; the prior-inventions page carries an unfilled e-signature field tag ("candidate_sig"), a leftover from a signing platform used for new hires.
