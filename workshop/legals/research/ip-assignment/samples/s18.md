## Document
- Shape: one EDGAR exhibit (EX-10.1 to a 10-K) holding a full acquisition-style agreement and its attachments. The main agreement is a title, a date line, a three-party block (assignor, assignee, intervenor), lettered recitals, a consideration line, then ten numbered articles (Interpretation; Purchase and Sale; Representations and Warranties; Survival; Covenants; Conditions; Closing; Indemnification; Intervention; General Provisions) with decimal sections down to three levels (5.1.12), a "[SIGNATURE PAGE FOLLOWS]" marker and a signature page. Attached in this order: Schedule 1.1(gg) Specifications (a lab-analysis table, two examples, partly in French); Schedule 2.2(a) Escrow Agreement (a full 20-clause three-party agreement with the law firm as escrow agent); an executed "Assignment and Moral Rights Waiver" deed dated about five months later, made at closing; and an executed addendum amending one definition. No prior-inventions list, no state-law notice, no DTSA notice, no work-for-hire clause, no future-inventions or holdover clause.
- Register: formal Canadian (Quebec) commercial drafting in English, third person with capitalised defined terms ("Assignor", "Assignee", "Intervenor", "Purchased Assets", "Technique"); long lists of synonyms ("claims, demands, complaints, grievances, actions..."); civil-law terms ("resiliated", "surety", "renouncing to the benefit of discussion and division", "Me" for lawyers). Typos and conversion slips ("m Section", "Recitals;;", "5.1.l l", "under under", the founder's surname spelled two ways).
- Recurring elements: defined terms in quotes and brackets with a 38-item definitions list; cross-references to section numbers throughout; page numbers; amounts in words and figures in Canadian dollars; each attached document has its own party block, recitals and signature block; "/s/" signatures; bilingual language clause in both the main agreement and the escrow agreement.
- Signer and context: the founder-inventor (a chemist) signs as individual assignor, "acting both personally and for a corporation to be incorporated"; the assignee is the company's predecessor holding company, signed by its president; the president also signs personally as intervenor, binding himself to the confidentiality and non-compete undertakings and standing surety for the company's ones. Given for money, not shares or employment: a fixed purchase price paid partly through escrow, partly at closing and partly in milestone payments tied to plant output, a consulting payment during a transition period, and royalties on products and licences up to a cap. Assigns one existing invention (a chemical process) and its IP, not future work. The later deed is signed by the founder and the numbered company he incorporated after signing; the addendum records that transfer. Host document: none; the IP assignment is the whole exhibit.
- Form origin: an executed copy, not a "Form of": names, date, amounts and "/s/" signatures filled in; one blank left for the assignor's address in the party block. Built on a Canadian asset-purchase template (materiality, closing certificates, conditions, indemnity procedure, Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act and Income Tax Act references) adapted to a single technique. The assignee's counsel, a named Montreal law firm, appears as escrow agent and notice copy; the assignor's counsel is also named. No document number or law-firm footer; the HTML title is the EDGAR file name.
