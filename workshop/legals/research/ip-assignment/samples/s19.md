## Document
- Shape: the exhibit is an executive employment agreement (23 numbered sections, signed by the CEO and the executive) with two exhibits: Exhibit A, a form of release to sign after a termination without cause (not part of the sample, except one paragraph that names the PIIA, row H5); Exhibit B, the Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement (PIIA). The PIIA is a preamble and thirteen paragraphs with no numbers and no headings, a signature block, then its own "Exhibit A" (the same label as the host's release), which holds the prior-inventions list and all the invention terms: licence to prior inventions, assignment, the California § 2870 exclusion with the statute quoted, disclosure, work for hire, further assistance, power of attorney, waiver of infringement claims. The sample is the PIIA with its Exhibit A plus five host passages that point to it (rows H1-H5).
- Register: formal first person from the employee ("I agree", "I hereby assign"), long sentences, the company as "the Company". The body of the PIIA deals with confidentiality and covenants; inventions get one paragraph that defines the terms and points to Exhibit A. Drafting drift: the employee is called an "associate" (a retail word) in the non-solicit and even inside the quoted text of § 2870 subsection (a), while (b) keeps "employee"; the non-disparagement clause switches from "I" to "you"; the host calls the attachment "PIIA" in one section and "PIAA" in two others; the signature line calls it the "Proprietary Information and Invention Agreement"; the PIIA calls itself the entire agreement on its subject while the host's entire-agreement clause includes it; the host release carries unremoved drafting footnotes ("To be updated to reflect changes in law").
- Recurring elements: "Confidential Information" and "Inventions" defined in place, no definitions section; "during my employment and at all times thereafter" as the time frame for confidentiality and non-disparagement; surrender of company documents and property on request and at the end of employment, stated three times (paragraphs 6 and 9, and the host's Section 4); no governing law, notice or signature line for the company in the PIIA itself.
- Signer and context: an employee signs (here a newly hired Chief Commercial Officer, on an executive employment agreement); the host makes abiding by the PIIA a condition of continued employment, and the PIIA preamble names employment and pay as its consideration. Host document: the executive employment agreement, whose Section 5 (pointer, a carve-out for one work the executive keeps, and its own confidentiality covenant), Section 16 (entire agreement), Sections 19-20 (DTSA notice and whistleblower carve-out, both said to reach the PIAA) and the release form are rows H1-H5. The host also holds a non-compete running through the severance period (Section 6), a Delaware governing-law clause with a California Labor Code § 925 waiver (Section 13), and makes severance depend on compliance with Section 5; none of these names the PIIA, so they have no row.
- Form origin: no "Form of" label, no law-firm footer or document number. The company's name is written into the preamble; the prior-inventions list is filled in "[None.]" in brackets; the retail word "associate" and retail examples of confidential information (buying plans, store designs, roll out plans, leases) point to a company-wide form for store and office staff reused for an executive. The PIIA's at-will paragraph defers to "any written employment agreement", which fits a general staff form.
