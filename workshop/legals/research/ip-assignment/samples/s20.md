## Document
- Shape: an amended and restated offer letter (16 numbered clauses, signature page) with two exhibits: Exhibit A is the Proprietary Information and Inventions Assignment Agreement (the sample), Exhibit B a separate Agreement to Arbitrate (not extracted). The assignment has ten Roman-numbered articles, most split into lettered sub-sections with bold run-in headings, a closing acknowledgement paragraph, a two-column signature block, and Appendix 1 (prior-inventions list with checkboxes and a table for inventions withheld under prior confidentiality). No table of contents, no definitions article: terms are defined where first used.
- Register: first person "I" for the employee, third person "the Company"; formal US drafting with long sentences and long illustrative lists ("by way of illustration but not limitation"); capitalised defined terms in quotes. Some loose drafting: the agreement calls itself both "Proprietary Agreement" and "Agreement", gives "either party" remedies in a one-way text, and the third-party information sentence lacks its main verb.
- Recurring elements: page numbers; employee name repeated under each exhibit title; cross-references by article and letter (Section III.B, III.F); "President or other Designated Representative" as the company's authorising officer throughout, never defined.
- Signer and context: the company's CEO and co-founder signs as an employee; the assignment is required "as a condition of your continuing employment" by clause 7 of the host letter, and the assignment itself names the compensation paid as consideration. It is made effective from the first day of employment or of any earlier services, and assigns work done earlier for the company's predecessors or founders, so it doubles as a founder clean-up of prior work. Host document: an amended and restated offer letter, which also carries a during-service no-competing and no-hiring-assist clause (clause 14) and an at-will clause; the Agreement to Arbitrate attached as Exhibit B expressly covers IP and trade-secret claims under the assignment.
- Form origin: an executed text, not a "Form of": the signer's and officer's names and dates are filled in and signatures shown as /s/. No law-firm footer or document number. The text points to a California origin although the company is in New York (California governing law, Los Angeles County court and arbitration seat, Labor Code § 2870 quoted), and the entire-agreement clause speaks of the company's "standard form" of the agreement, so it is the company's house form adapted with the signer's name.
