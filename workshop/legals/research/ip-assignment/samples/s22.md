## Document
- Shape: an unnumbered offer letter (three pages, signature and acceptance block) with two exhibits: Exhibit A is the Proprietary Information and Invention Assignment Agreement (the sample), Exhibit B a Change in Control and Severance Agreement (not extracted beyond the three clauses that point to the assignment). The assignment has nine numbered sections, sections 1, 2 and 6 split into lettered sub-sections with run-in headings, a separate signature page with addresses, and its own "Exhibit A" (prior-inventions checkboxes). No table of contents, no definitions section: terms are defined where first used, and section 6 opens with its own definitions sub-section.
- Register: first person "I" for the employee, third person "the Company"; formal US drafting, long lists of IP and information kinds, capitalised defined terms in quotes. Some loose drafting: the prior-inventions schedule is called "Exhibit A" inside a document that is itself Exhibit A; "my Relationship with the Company" is capitalised but never defined; "this Agreement" and "this agreement" are mixed; the interpretation sentence defines "including" as "including", having lost its "without limitation".
- Recurring elements: page numbers on each page; a Docusign envelope ID footer on every page; cross-references by section and letter (Section 1(b), 2(b), 3); "supervisor or any officer" and "an authorized officer" as the company's contact points, never named.
- Signer and context: an incoming Chief Financial Officer signs as an employee; the letter makes signing and complying with the assignment a condition of employment, and the assignment names entering employment and the compensation paid as consideration. The assignment covers inventions made during employment only (no reach-back to earlier services, no post-employment holdover period). Host document: an offer letter that also carries a no-conflicting-duty representation, an outside-activities rule, an at-will clause and an integration clause naming the assignment; the attached severance agreement confirms the assignment's obligations, keeps IP disputes out of arbitration, and lists the assignment in its own whole-agreement clause. Not present anywhere in the file: a Defend Trade Secrets Act immunity notice, a state invention-assignment-limit notice, a whistleblower or NLRA carve-out, a moral-rights waiver, a holdover clause.
- Form origin: an executed text sent through Docusign, not a "Form of": the signer's name is filled in throughout, the CEO's name in the company block, and the letter dated. The offer letter and the severance agreement carry a law-firm-style document-management number ("US-DOCS\" plus a number) in their footers; the assignment pages carry none. Pennsylvania governing law matches the company's Pittsburgh headquarters. The title, "Proprietary Information and Invention Assignment Agreement", is one shared with other forms in the pool.
