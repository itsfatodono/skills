- Register: Midwestern US small-company buy-sell boilerplate: long sentences, "hereby", "hereto", "notwithstanding", some numbers in words with the figure in parentheses, most only in figures. Clearly cut down from a fuller buy-sell template, leaving many dangling pieces: the Section 1 heading promises restrictions on "Issuance" but none follow; Section 2 cross-refers to "Subsection 3(b)" and "this Section 3" for its own sub-clauses and to "Section 8" for the closing, which is Section 3; Section 3 sets a closing date for "a purchase pursuant to Section 4" after a deceased holder's estate gets a representative, but Section 4 is the co-sale and no death buy-out exists; Section 4 refers to a "Transfer Notice referred to in Section 3", a term never defined (Section 2 calls it the "Offer"); the legend mentions repurchase rights "upon termination of service", which the agreement does not grant; Section 7(b) lets holders buy back life and disability policies held "for funding the purchase of Shares", though no insurance-funded buy-out is left; the miscellaneous clauses speak of "either party" and "the other party" in a four-party agreement; 8(i) runs (a)–(c) inside a lettered clause; 8(o) repeats "to the capital".
- Recurring elements: every right runs first to the company and then to the other shareholders pro rata; every governance change in Section 8 needs all shareholders; holders' names are written straight into the operative clauses (salary, board), not in a schedule.
