## Document
- Version taken: the live page returns 403 to fetching. Latest Wayback capture: https://web.archive.org/web/20261015184728/https://www.adobe.com/legal/subscription-terms.html. That capture is a shell page whose body loads one content fragment, `/cc-shared/fragments/legal/subscription/full-content` (geo-routing off, so the default US English version). The fragment text was taken from Adobe's own AEM origin (`main--da-cc--adobecom.aem.live/cc-shared/fragments/legal/subscription/full-content.plain.html`) because the same fragment on adobe.com also returns 403. Effective date August 1st, 2026. There is also an earlier Wayback capture of the fragment from 2026-09-01.
- Placement: separate page, "Adobe Subscription and Cancellation Terms". The whole page is the part. It covers Creative Cloud, Document Cloud, Firefly, Acrobat and Frame "for United States", Substance, and Adobe Stock.
- Words: about 23,000 in the part, which is the whole page body (site header and footer not counted). Most of it repeats the same paragraphs once for every plan; there are only about 9,000 words of unique paragraphs.
- Billing channels: Adobe bills directly through stored payment methods (card, PayPal, direct debit, bank transfer, convenience-store payment, purchase order with invoice, boleto in Brazil). Payment processors close FX contracts for Brazil and Latin America. Prepaid cards and redemption codes (POSA, EDUCARD) turn into auto-renewing subscriptions once a payment method is added. Reseller and VIP buyers are pointed to their reseller. Apple, Google and other app stores are not mentioned, even though the candidate came from the Acrobat Reader app listing.
- Shape: one long page built as a nested accordion matrix. H1 title, effective date, then an H2 per product family, an H3 per audience or channel (individuals, education, teams, EDUCARD, redemption/POSA, resellers, legacy products; for Stock: on-demand, free trials, month to month, annual), and an H4 accordion per plan type (41 in all: month to month, annual billed monthly, annual prepaid, direct debit or bank transfer, purchase order, multi-year, with or without rollover). Each accordion holds two or three labelled blocks, "Free Trial Terms" (or "Free Trial and Subscription Terms"), "Subscription Terms" and "Cancellation Terms", in that order. No table of contents, no intro, no definitions section, no tables.
- Register: plain consumer style with "we/you" and short-to-medium sentences. Plan facts are stated directly, with few hedges apart from "may change your plan's rate" and "at Adobe's sole discretion". There are no defined terms except "Credits" (Substance) and quota. Regional rules are written as "For customers in X, ..." sentences.
- Recurring elements: the same paragraphs are copied into every accordion: payment-method authorisation, price-change notice, failed-payment/backup-method, generative credits, education eligibility, authority to bind an employer, EEA authentication, Brazil and Latin America FX. The links to the Adobe Account plan page and Customer Support appear in nearly every block (about 120 links). The 14-day full-refund window is repeated in every cancellation block. California, Brazil, Argentina and Latin America cancellation variants are added under the default cancellation paragraph.
- Coverage notes: there is no general preamble, governing-law clause or link to the General Terms. There is no app-store purchase clause and no gift subscription. Plan changes appear only as team seat additions and Stock "lesser value subscription" forfeiture; there is no upgrade/downgrade clause. The page has no statutory renewal-notice text of its own (it says "where required by applicable law"), except for Australia. It has no withdrawal form. Consumable credits and quota (generative credits, Substance asset credits, Stock quota and credit packs) sit inside the subscription terms.
