## Document
- Shape: one Terms and Conditions page (effective July 29, 2026); H1 title, effective date, two intro paragraphs, then 10 numbered top-level sections with decimal sub-clauses (1.1, 2.1.a). The subscription terms are spread over four places: a definition clause (1.2), a free-trial clause inside the usage section (2.6), all of section 3 "Payment Obligations" and all of section 4 "Term and Termination". No summary box, no table of contents, no tables.
- Placement: part of ToS. Headings: "1.2. Subscriptions"; "2.6. Free Tools and Services"; "3. Payment Obligations" (3.1 Payment Terms, 3.2 Billing Policies, 3.3 Payment Information, 3.4 Cancellation); "4. Term and Termination" (4.1–4.6). Sits early to middle: a clause of section 1, a clause of section 2, then sections 3–4 of 10.
- Words: subscription part about 2,150 (the clauses listed above); whole page about 8,100 (body text, excluding site navigation and footer).
- Register: B2B SaaS contract style addressed to a "Customer" (an individual or the organisation they sign up for), switching between "Customer" and "you"; defined terms in quotes ("Subscription Period", "Payment Method", "Payment Processor", "Free Account", "Taxes"); key consumer-facing rules (trial conversion, cancellation, no refunds) in ALL CAPS; numbered (i)/(ii) and (a)/(b) inline lists.
- Recurring elements: notice periods stated as words plus digits ("twenty-four (24) hours"); cross-references to other sections ("SECTION 4 OF THESE TERMS") and to the Privacy Policy; "except as expressly stated in the Terms" carve-outs on refunds; previous-version dates listed at the page end.
- Billing channels: billing is by the company through named third-party Payment Processors (RevenueCat for in-app payments, Stripe for other payments), with links to their terms; the subscription part does not name Apple or Google as the biller, has no store-cancellation or store-refund instructions, and does not say store purchases follow the store's terms. A separate general clause (10.11 Additional Terms for Mobile Applications) covers App Store and Google Play only as licence/third-party-beneficiary terms, with Apple refunding the app purchase price on a warranty failure.
- Coverage notes: written for a business customer (invoicing by email, organisations, EU Data Act switching) applied to all users including individual app subscribers; no plan list or plan contents; no upgrade path beyond a downgrade-to-free clause; no failed-payment/suspension clause; no gift or family plans; no US state auto-renewal law named.
